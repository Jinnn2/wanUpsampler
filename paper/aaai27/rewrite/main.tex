\begin{abstract}
High-resolution video diffusion remains expensive because every denoising
evaluation processes the full target-resolution spatiotemporal latent
sequence. In-trajectory resolution scaling reduces this cost by executing an
inexpensive low-resolution prefix and reserving target-resolution computation
for the remaining evaluations, without launching a separate super-resolution
diffusion process or increasing the total denoising-evaluation budget.
However, fixed interpolation cannot recover the VAE-induced cross-resolution
latent correspondence, while a learned upsampler faces a training--inference
mismatch between completed clean latents and predictions from an unfinished
trajectory. We introduce InTraScale, a decoupled framework that addresses
these challenges while keeping the pretrained generator frozen. Its
In-Trajectory Upsampler learns the cross-resolution correspondence from
content-aligned clean latent pairs, whereas its Trajectory-Tail Distillation
Adapter distills the omitted low-resolution trajectory tail into the
transition evaluation to produce a more endpoint-like input. A
scheduler-consistent state reconstruction then returns the prediction to the
remaining target-resolution trajectory. On a 50-evaluation Wan2.1 sampler,
InTraScale achieves \(3.21\times\) and \(4.44\times\) warm-model speedups
while retaining 97.76\% and 97.53\% of the Native-HR VBench-5 score,
respectively. On a distilled four-evaluation model, it provides an additional
\(2.13\times\) acceleration while retaining 99.58\% of the native distilled
score. These results establish in-trajectory resolution scaling as an
effective spatial acceleration strategy complementary to timestep
distillation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Recent video diffusion models generate increasingly coherent motion and
fine-grained visual content, but their computational cost remains a major
obstacle to practical deployment~\citep{wan2025}. Each denoising evaluation
applies a large diffusion transformer to the complete spatiotemporal latent
sequence, making target-resolution generation particularly expensive.
Timestep distillation reduces the number of evaluations, but every retained
evaluation still processes the full target-resolution grid. Spatial
acceleration is therefore complementary to temporal acceleration: global
layout, semantic structure, and motion can be established economically at low
resolution, while expensive target-resolution computation is reserved for
recovering local structure and high-frequency details.

Existing low-to-high-resolution pipelines commonly follow a post-generation
schedule: a low-resolution generator first completes its trajectory, after
which an upsampling or super-resolution stage increases the output
resolution~\citep{ho2022imagenvideo,blattmann2023videoldm,wang2023lavie}.
RGB-space pipelines may incur codec and state-reconstruction overhead when the
enhanced video is returned to a generative model for further refinement,
whereas latent cascades avoid this conversion by upsampling the completed
low-resolution representation directly~\citep{xie2025simplegvr,
zhao2026luve}. Despite this difference, both schedules append refinement
after completing low-resolution generation. In-trajectory resolution scaling
instead performs the resolution transition before the low-resolution
trajectory is complete and uses the remaining denoising evaluations as the
high-resolution continuation~\citep{jeong2025ralu,lightx2v2026}. It therefore
accelerates generation without launching a separate super-resolution
diffusion process or increasing the original denoising-evaluation budget.

Existing in-trajectory approaches primarily rely on prescribed latent
interpolation to perform the resolution transition. Such interpolation only
resamples the spatial grid and cannot recover the cross-resolution
correspondence induced by the pretrained VAE. The resulting structural
distortion becomes particularly difficult to correct under a late transition,
where only a short high-resolution continuation remains. A natural alternative
is to replace interpolation with a learned latent upsampler. However, the
upsampler is typically trained using completed, content-aligned clean latent
pairs, whereas in-trajectory inference provides a clean prediction derived
from an unfinished low-resolution trajectory. This prediction retains a
timestep-dependent residual that the omitted low-resolution trajectory tail
would otherwise remove. Directly applying the learned upsampler therefore
forces it to perform both cross-resolution reconstruction and unfinished
trajectory correction, creating a training--inference mismatch. These two
functions should consequently be modeled separately.

We introduce \textbf{InTraScale}, a decoupled framework for learned
in-trajectory resolution scaling. The \textbf{In-Trajectory Upsampler (ITU)}
learns the VAE-specific correspondence between completed clean low- and
high-resolution latent pairs, replacing geometry-only interpolation with
learned cross-resolution reconstruction. The \textbf{Trajectory-Tail
Distillation Adapter (TTDA)} separately distills the omitted low-resolution
trajectory tail into the transition evaluation, producing a more
endpoint-like clean prediction for ITU. InTraScale then reconstructs the next
target-resolution scheduler state and executes the remaining evaluations
using the frozen flow predictor. On conventional 50-evaluation Wan2.1
sampling, InTraScale achieves \(3.21\times\) and \(4.44\times\) warm-model
speedups while retaining 97.76\% and 97.53\% of the Native-HR VBench-5 score,
respectively. Applied to a distilled four-evaluation model, it provides an
additional \(2.13\times\) acceleration while retaining 99.58\% of the native
distilled-model score.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce InTraScale, a learned in-trajectory resolution-scaling
  framework that distributes a fixed denoising-evaluation budget between an
  inexpensive low-resolution prefix and a high-resolution continuation.

  \item We decouple resolution conversion from unfinished trajectory
  correction: ITU learns the VAE-specific cross-resolution clean-latent
  correspondence, while TTDA distills the omitted low-resolution trajectory
  tail into the transition evaluation.

  \item We evaluate InTraScale on conventional 50-evaluation and distilled
  four-evaluation Wan2.1 samplers, demonstrating substantial spatial
  acceleration both before and after temporal distillation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Post-Generation Resolution Enhancement.}
Post-generation approaches increase resolution after a low-resolution output
has been completed. RGB-space methods such as Upscale-A-Video, VideoGigaGAN,
and VEnhancer improve spatial detail and temporal consistency in decoded
videos~\citep{zhou2024upscale,xu2024videogigagan,he2024venhancer}. MrFlow
combines pixel-space super-resolution with subsequent high-resolution
refinement for image flow-matching models~\citep{zheng2026mrflow}. Latent
cascades instead upsample the completed low-resolution representation
directly: SimpleGVR uses an efficient cascaded video super-resolution model,
while LUVE further introduces frequency-specialized
refinement~\citep{xie2025simplegvr,zhao2026luve}. Although latent processing
avoids codec conversion, both categories place resolution enhancement after
the low-resolution trajectory has reached its endpoint.

\paragraph{In-Trajectory Resolution Scaling.}
Another line of work changes spatial resolution within the generative
trajectory. Pyramidal Flow Matching jointly trains a unified video DiT over
multiple resolution stages~\citep{jin2025pyramidalflow}. RALU applies
region-adaptive latent expansion and noise--timestep rescheduling to
pretrained diffusion transformers, while LightX2V supports
changing-resolution inference for pretrained video
models~\citep{jeong2025ralu,lightx2v2026}. More recent approaches improve
cross-resolution trajectory construction: Fresco combines a unified noise
field with variance-guided progressive expansion, whereas Spectral
Progressive Diffusion derives noise expansion and resolution schedules in the
spectral domain~\citep{zheng2026fresco,xiao2026spectral}. These methods either
train the generator around a multiscale trajectory or rely on prescribed
spatial and noise transformations. InTraScale instead keeps the generator
frozen, learns the VAE-specific clean-latent correspondence, and separately
distills the omitted low-resolution trajectory tail.

\paragraph{Video Diffusion Acceleration.}
Video consistency and diffusion distillation reduce the number of denoising
evaluations~\citep{wang2023videolcm,li2024t2vturbo,ding2025dollar}, while
caching methods reuse attention maps or intermediate features across
timesteps~\citep{zhao2025pab,liu2025teacache}. InTraScale reduces spatial
computation during the low-resolution prefix and can therefore provide
additional acceleration to independently distilled or cached generators.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Challenges of In-Trajectory Transition}
In-trajectory resolution scaling executes an inexpensive low-resolution
prefix and reserves the target resolution for only the remaining denoising
evaluations. Its central technical difficulty lies in the resolution
transition between the two trajectory segments. Although this strategy avoids
launching a separate super-resolution diffusion process, we identify two
transition-specific challenges that become particularly important when the
transition is placed late in the denoising trajectory to maximize
acceleration.

\paragraph{Interpolation-induced latent distortion.}
A fixed interpolation operator only resamples the spatial grid; it does not
recover the correspondence between the low- and high-resolution representations
induced by the pretrained VAE. Consequently, interpolated latents may exhibit
distorted structures and inaccurate high-frequency statistics on the
target-resolution grid. RALU reported that late latent upsampling introduces
aliasing artifacts, particularly around high-frequency edge regions. This
exposes a fundamental quality–efficiency trade-off: a later resolution
transition provides greater acceleration but leaves fewer high-resolution
evaluations to correct the errors introduced during resolution conversion.
With only a short high-resolution suffix, the remaining target-resolution
evaluations may therefore be insufficient to recover structural details lost
or distorted by fixed interpolation.

\paragraph{Training–inference mismatch for learned upsampling.}
Replacing interpolation with a learned latent upsampler introduces an input
distribution shift. A latent upsampler is naturally trained using paired clean
latents obtained from completed, content-aligned low- and high-resolution
videos. Its low-resolution training input is thus an endpoint-like clean
representation containing all spatial and temporal details supported by the
low-resolution bandwidth. During in-trajectory inference, however, the
upsampler does not receive such a converged latent. Here, \(x_s^L\) denotes
the low-resolution latent state at step \(s\), \(v_\phi\) is the frozen flow
predictor with parameters \(\phi\), \(c\) is the text condition, and
\(\sigma_s\) is the scheduler coefficient. We use the subscript \(0|s\) to
denote a clean latent predicted from the state at step \(s\). For a
resolution transition after evaluation \(s\), the upsampler receives a
single-evaluation clean prediction derived from an unfinished low-resolution
trajectory:
\[
\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{L}
=
x_s^{L}
-
\sigma_s v_\phi(x_s^{L},s,c).
\]
It contains a timestep- and scheduler-dependent residual that would otherwise
be removed by the remaining low-resolution evaluations. Its distribution
therefore differs from that of the clean low-resolution latents used to train
the upsampler. Directly applying the upsampler to this prediction forces a
single module to perform both cross-resolution reconstruction and unfinished
trajectory correction, weakening its ability to preserve structure across
scales.

These two challenges motivate the decoupled design of InTraScale. The
In-Trajectory Upsampler learns the cross-resolution clean-latent
correspondence, while the Trajectory-Tail Distillation Adapter distills the
omitted low-resolution trajectory tail into the final low-resolution
evaluation, producing a more endpoint-like input for the subsequent
resolution transition.

\subsection{Overall Framework}

Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework} summarizes the sampling budget and the
decoupled pipeline motivated by the two challenges above. We now formalize
its components and trajectory states.
Let \(v_\phi(x_k^R,k,c)\) denote the frozen flow predictor, where
\(R\in\{L,H\}\) indicates the low- or target-resolution latent space,
\(x_k^R\) is the latent state presented to the \(k\)-th denoising evaluation,
and \(c\) is the text condition. We use \(\sigma_k\) for the corresponding
scheduler coefficient and \(T\) for the total number of denoising evaluations.
We refer to the pretrained flow predictor and VAE collectively as the frozen
target model.
We define a resolution transition after evaluation \(s\). The \(s\)-th
evaluation is therefore the final evaluation executed on the low-resolution
grid. Evaluations \(1,\ldots,s-1\) form the frozen low-resolution prefix,
TTDA is activated only during evaluation \(s\), and evaluations
\(s+1,\ldots,T\) form the frozen high-resolution continuation.

During the final low-resolution evaluation, the prediction
\(\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{L}\) introduced in the preceding subsection is not
directly passed to the upsampler. The Trajectory-Tail Distillation Adapter
(TTDA) first produces an endpoint-aligned clean prediction
\(\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L}\). The In-Trajectory Upsampler (ITU), parameterized
by \(\psi\), then maps this prediction to the target-resolution clean latent
\(\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{H}=U_\psi(\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L})\).

To continue the original denoising trajectory, InTraScale reconstructs the
next target-resolution scheduler state as
\[
x_{s+1}^{H}
=
(1-\sigma_{s+1})\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{H}
+
\sigma_{s+1}\epsilon^{H},
\qquad
\epsilon^{H}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),
\]
where \(\epsilon^{H}\) is target-resolution noise determined by the generation
seed. The complete inference pipeline is
\[
x_1^{L}
\xrightarrow{\text{LR}}
x_s^{L}
\xrightarrow{\text{TTDA}}
\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L}
\xrightarrow{\text{ITU}}
\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{H}
\xrightarrow{\text{reconstruction}}
x_{s+1}^{H}
\xrightarrow{\text{HR}}
z_{\mathrm{out}}^{H}.
\]
Here, \(z_{\mathrm{out}}^{H}\) denotes the final target-resolution clean latent,
which is decoded into the output video by the frozen VAE.

ITU and TTDA are trained independently using supervision generated by the
frozen target model. ITU learns the cross-resolution clean-latent
correspondence, whereas TTDA learns to remove the unfinished
low-resolution trajectory residual at the transition point.

\subsection{In-Trajectory Upsampler}
\label{sec:itu}

The In-Trajectory Upsampler (ITU) addresses the cross-resolution representation
mismatch identified in Section~3.1. Rather than treating latent upsampling as
spatial interpolation, ITU learns the correspondence between clean
low- and high-resolution representations induced by the frozen target model.

\paragraph{Generator-endogenous supervision.}
We construct the training pairs using videos generated by the frozen target
model. Given a generated target-resolution video \(v^{H}\), we first obtain its
content-aligned low-resolution counterpart through RGB-space downsampling:
\[
v^{L}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rgb}}(v^{H}).
\]
The two videos are then encoded independently by the same frozen VAE
\(\mathcal{E}\):
\[
z_{\mathrm{pair}}^{L}=\mathcal{E}(v^{L}),
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{pair}}^{H}=\mathcal{E}(v^{H}).
\]
The resulting pair
\((z_{\mathrm{pair}}^{L},z_{\mathrm{pair}}^{H})\) shares the same semantics,
frame count, and motion trajectory. Performing the resolution change before
VAE encoding allows ITU to learn the target model's actual cross-resolution
latent correspondence instead of imitating a predefined interpolation
operator in latent space.

\paragraph{Spatiotemporal latent upsampling.}
ITU maps a clean low-resolution latent to the target-resolution latent grid
while preserving the temporal dimension:
\[
U_\psi:
\mathbb{R}^{B\times16\times F\times h_L\times w_L}
\rightarrow
\mathbb{R}^{B\times16\times F\times h_H\times w_H},
\]
where \(B\) and \(F\) denote the batch size and number of latent frames,
respectively. The network follows an encoder--resampler--decoder design. A
3D convolution first projects the input into a 256-channel hidden
representation, followed by four spatiotemporal residual blocks. A learned
spatial lifting operator then changes only the height and width. Four
additional residual blocks and a final 3D convolution reconstruct the
16-channel target latent. The 3D blocks allow adjacent latent frames to
contribute to the reconstruction without changing the temporal resolution.

We instantiate the spatial lifting operator according to the required grid
ratio. For \(480\times832\rightarrow720\times1248\), the latent grid changes
from \(60\times104\) to \(90\times156\). We realize this \(3/2\) ratio using a
\(3\times\) spatial PixelShuffle followed by anti-aliased stride-2
downsampling. For
\(368\times640\rightarrow720\times1248\), a \(2\times\) PixelShuffle produces
a \(92\times160\) grid, which is center-cropped to \(90\times156\). The
resolution-specific lifting operators determine the target geometry, while
the surrounding spatiotemporal network learns the latent correspondence.

\paragraph{Cross-resolution objective.}
Given
\[
\widehat{z}_{\mathrm{pair}}^{H}
=
U_\psi(z_{\mathrm{pair}}^{L}),
\]
We train ITU with
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ITU}}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}
+0.2\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lf}}
+0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temp}}.
\]
Here, \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}\) is the Charbonnier reconstruction loss,
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lf}}\) enforces consistency between the spatially
downsampled prediction and the low-resolution input, and
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temp}}\) matches first-order temporal differences
between adjacent latent frames. We use a Charbonnier constant of
\(\epsilon=10^{-3}\).
Here, \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{lat}}\) maps the prediction back to the
low-resolution latent grid, and \(\Delta_t\) denotes first-order differences
between adjacent latent frames. We use
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{lf}}=0.2\),
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{temp}}=0.1\), and
\(\epsilon=10^{-3}\). The three terms constrain target-latent reconstruction,
low-frequency content preservation, and temporal evolution, respectively.

A low-resolution latent does not uniquely determine every high-frequency
detail in its target-resolution counterpart. ITU therefore focuses on
transferring structure, motion, and latent statistics to the target grid. The
remaining frozen high-resolution trajectory retains the generative
responsibility for details that are underdetermined by the low-resolution
input.

\subsection{Trajectory-Tail Distillation and High-Resolution Continuation}
\label{sec:ttda}

ITU is trained on completed clean-latent pairs, whereas its inference input is
predicted from an unfinished low-resolution trajectory. TTDA reduces this
deployment mismatch by distilling the omitted low-resolution trajectory tail
into the final low-resolution evaluation. The aligned prediction is
subsequently lifted by ITU and returned to the native high-resolution
schedule.

\paragraph{Trajectory-tail endpoint supervision.}
For each training sample, we run the frozen target model using a fixed prompt,
generation seed, initial noise, scheduler, and guidance configuration. We
cache the state \(x_s^{L}\) immediately before the final low-resolution
evaluation and continue the same low-resolution trajectory to completion. We
denote the resulting clean endpoint by \(z_{\mathrm{end}}^{L}\). The pair
\[
\left(x_s^{L},z_{\mathrm{end}}^{L}\right)
\]
therefore preserves the semantic and stochastic conditions at the
transition point while providing direct supervision for the trajectory
residual remaining at evaluation \(s\).

\paragraph{Localized trajectory-tail distillation.}
TTDA is implemented as a step-specific low-rank update
\(\Delta\theta_s\) to the frozen flow predictor. For a linear layer \(W\), the
LoRA parameterization is
\[
W_{\lambda}
=
W
+
\lambda\frac{\alpha}{r}BA,
\]
where \(r\) is the LoRA rank, \(\alpha\) is its training scale, and
\(\lambda\) controls the adapter strength during inference. We use rank
\(r=32\) and inject the update into the \(q\), \(k\), \(v\), and \(o\)
attention projections and the two feed-forward projections. The base
parameters \(\phi\) remain frozen.

At evaluation \(s\), the adapted predictor produces
\[
\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L}
=
x_s^{L}
-
\sigma_s
v_{\phi+\lambda\Delta\theta_s}
(x_s^{L},s,c).
\]
We train TTDA to regress the corresponding native low-resolution endpoint
using
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TTDA}}
=
\mathcal{L}_{1}
+
0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{tt}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temp}}.
\]
Here, \(\mathcal{L}_{1}\) and \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}\) compare
\(\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L}\) with \(z_{\mathrm{end}}^{L}\), while
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temp}}\) matches their first-order temporal
differences. We set \(\lambda_{\mathrm{tt}}=0.05\) for the earlier
transition after evaluation \(s=40\) in the 50-step sampler and zero for
the later and distilled settings. We select the inference strength
\(\lambda=0.75\) on held-out
development prompts and keep it fixed for all reported samples.

TTDA is activated only during evaluation \(s\). Its strength is zero for the
preceding evaluations, so the frozen low-resolution prefix and the cached
pre-transition state \(x_s^{L}\) remain unchanged under matched
deterministic conditions. TTDA is therefore a localized trajectory adapter
rather than a general low-resolution step-reduction model.

\paragraph{Scheduler-consistent high-resolution continuation.}
After TTDA and ITU produce
\[
\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{H}
=
U_\psi(\widetilde{z}_{0|s}^{L}),
\]
we reconstruct the next target-resolution scheduler state as
\[
x_{s+1}^{H}
=
(1-\sigma_{s+1})
\widehat{z}_{0|s}^{H}
+
\sigma_{s+1}\epsilon^{H},
\]
using the target-resolution noise realization \(\epsilon^{H}\) determined by
the generation seed. This reconstruction is parameter-free: it places the
upsampled clean prediction at the noise level expected by the next evaluation
without introducing an auxiliary diffusion process or a decode--encode
round trip.

We require \(s<T\), leaving at least one target-resolution evaluation after
the resolution transition. The frozen flow predictor then processes
\(x_{s+1}^{H}\) through evaluations \(s+1,\ldots,T\) and produces
\(z_{\mathrm{out}}^{H}\). State reconstruction itself does not synthesize new
detail; it only provides a scheduler-consistent entry into the
target-resolution trajectory. The frozen high-resolution tail performs the
actual generative refinement, completing spatial structures and textures that
cannot be uniquely recovered from the low-resolution latent.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

We evaluate InTraScale on both the standard 50-evaluation Wan2.1 T2V-1.3B
sampler and a four-evaluation Wan2.1 T2V-14B StepDistill-CfgDistill sampler.
This section first examines the 50-evaluation setting, where resolution
transitions are placed after evaluation 40 or 45. We additionally evaluate
fixed interpolation after evaluations 40, 45, and 48 to characterize the
effect of increasingly delayed transitions. All configurations generate
81-frame videos at \(720\times1248\) resolution from matched text prompts,
random seeds, initial noise, schedulers, and guidance settings.

We evaluate generation quality on ten matched prompts using VBench. Our primary
aggregate metric, VBench-5, is the unweighted mean of subject consistency,
background consistency, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, and imaging
quality. Dynamic degree is reported separately because a larger motion
magnitude does not necessarily indicate better quality. Prompt-level
differences are analyzed using paired bootstrap confidence intervals and
two-sided exact sign tests.

We measure efficiency using single-video warm-model end-to-end latency. Each
configuration is loaded once, followed by one warm-up video and five measured
videos in the same resident process. Measurements are CUDA-synchronized and
include all per-video denoising evaluations, TTDA and ITU execution,
scheduler-state reconstruction, VAE processing, and output operations.
One-time runner construction and checkpoint loading are excluded.

\subsection{Overall Quality--Efficiency Trade-off}
\label{sec:quality_efficiency}

We compare InTraScale with three classes of alternatives. Native-HR executes
all 50 evaluations at the target resolution. Fixed Interpolation directly
resamples the latent grid at different transition points. RALU-style
rescheduling adjusts the noise--timestep correspondence at the representative
transition after evaluation 45. Finally, Endpoint-ITU completes all 50
evaluations at low resolution, applies ITU to the resulting endpoint, and
performs five additional high-resolution refinement evaluations.



InTraScale@40 reduces warm-model latency from 187.54 to 58.49 seconds,
corresponding to a \(3.21\times\) speedup, while retaining 97.76\% of the
Native-HR VBench-5 score. Delaying the transition to evaluation 45 further
reduces latency to 42.26 seconds, achieving a \(4.44\times\) speedup and
retaining 97.53\% of the Native-HR score. Moving the transition from
evaluation 40 to 45 reduces latency by an additional 27.74\%, with a VBench-5
decrease of only 0.00191. The two operating points therefore provide a
controllable quality--efficiency trade-off.

At matched transition positions, the learned transition introduces little
additional latency over fixed interpolation. InTraScale@40 and InTraScale@45
add only 0.63\% and 0.67\% latency, respectively, while improving VBench-5 by
0.03171 and 0.04016. The widening quality gap at the later transition supports
the challenge identified in Section~3.1: as the high-resolution continuation
becomes shorter, fixed interpolation leaves insufficient evaluations to repair
the induced latent distortion.

At evaluation 45, InTraScale also improves VBench-5 over RALU-style
rescheduling by 0.00352 with a 0.61\% latency increase. This comparison
suggests that scheduler adaptation alone does not fully recover the
cross-resolution latent correspondence addressed by ITU.

The Endpoint-ITU baseline completes the entire low-resolution trajectory
before resolution conversion and adds five high-resolution refinements.
Despite executing these additional evaluations, it reaches only 0.80073
VBench-5 at 44.00 seconds. InTraScale@45 is 3.96\% faster and improves
VBench-5 by 0.00719. Transitioning before the low-resolution endpoint therefore
provides a more favorable operating point than completing low-resolution
generation and subsequently launching a separate high-resolution refinement
suffix.

Overall, InTraScale@40 and InTraScale@45 establish two non-dominated operating
points between Native-HR quality and aggressive low-resolution execution. The
prompt-paired confidence intervals against Native-HR overlap zero, but the
evaluation does not constitute an equivalence test. We therefore characterize
InTraScale as a quality--efficiency frontier rather than claiming lossless
acceleration.

\subsection{Further Accelerating Distilled Video Generation}
\label{sec:distilled_acceleration}

Although Section~\ref{sec:quality_efficiency} demonstrates substantial
acceleration for conventional 50-evaluation sampling, deployment-oriented
video generators increasingly rely on timestep-distilled models to reduce the
trajectory length. We therefore ask whether in-trajectory resolution scaling
can provide additional gains after temporal computation has already been
compressed. To this end, we apply InTraScale to a four-evaluation Wan2.1
T2V-14B StepDistill-CfgDistill model. The resolution transition occurs after
the third low-resolution evaluation, leaving only one target-resolution
evaluation for generative refinement.

We compare against Native-HR4, which executes all four evaluations at the
target resolution, and Trilinear@3, which uses the same
\(3\)-LR/\(1\)-HR schedule as InTraScale but replaces ITU with fixed
interpolation and disables TTDA. We further construct two controlled
post-generation baselines on the same distilled backbone. Both complete all
four low-resolution evaluations before increasing the spatial resolution.
LUVE-style then applies learned latent upsampling followed by two
high-resolution refinements, whereas MrFlow-style uses a
decode--RGB-upsample--re-encode path followed by one high-resolution
correction. These configurations reproduce the corresponding computational
patterns rather than the complete original systems.

\subsection{Component and Design Analysis}
\label{sec:component_analysis}

We next examine whether ITU and TTDA address the two transition-specific
challenges identified in Section~3.1. On 50 completed clean-latent pairs per
scale, ITU consistently improves cross-resolution reconstruction over
trilinear interpolation. For \(480\mathrm{p}\rightarrow720\mathrm{p}\), it
reduces latent \(L_1\), LPIPS, and temporal \(L_1\) by 48.6\%, 73.3\%, and
30.8\%, respectively. Under the more aggressive
\(368\mathrm{p}\rightarrow720\mathrm{p}\) conversion, the corresponding
reductions are 33.4\%, 51.1\%, and 15.5\%. These results confirm that ITU
learns the VAE-specific cross-resolution correspondence rather than merely
approximating geometric interpolation.

TTDA consistently brings the transition-point prediction closer to the endpoint of the
complete low-resolution trajectory. It reduces endpoint latent \(L_1\) by
25.82\%, 21.03\%, and 5.03\% at Wan50@40, Wan50@45, and Distill4@3,
respectively, with improvements on all ten samples in each setting. The
factorial evaluation further separates the roles of the two modules:
replacing trilinear interpolation with ITU improves VBench-5 by
0.03085--0.04066, whereas adding TTDA changes the aggregate score by only
\(-0.00050\) to \(+0.00087\). ITU therefore provides the dominant perceptual
gain, while TTDA primarily corrects the transition-specific endpoint mismatch.

Finally, we validate the target-resolution state reconstruction on an
independent eight-prompt set. Using seed-derived target-resolution noise
instead of resized low-resolution flow improves VBench-5 by 0.02483 and
temporal consistency by 0.01605, showing the importance of reconstructing a
scheduler-consistent high-resolution state. Performance remains stable when
the TTDA strength varies from 0.25 to 1.0; we select
\(\lambda=0.75\) on the validation set and keep it fixed for all test
evaluations. Full metric breakdowns and strength sweeps are provided in the
supplementary material.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}

We presented InTraScale, a learned in-trajectory resolution-scaling framework
that distributes a fixed denoising-evaluation budget between an inexpensive
low-resolution prefix and a target-resolution continuation. Unlike
post-generation cascades, InTraScale performs the resolution transition before
the low-resolution trajectory is completed and does not launch a separate
super-resolution diffusion process. ITU learns the VAE-specific
cross-resolution clean-latent correspondence, while TTDA distills the omitted
low-resolution trajectory tail to provide a more endpoint-like input for
resolution conversion. Scheduler-consistent state reconstruction then allows
the frozen generator to execute the remaining target-resolution evaluations.
On conventional 50-evaluation Wan2.1 sampling, InTraScale achieves
\(3.21\times\) and \(4.44\times\) acceleration while retaining 97.76\% and
97.53\% of the Native-HR VBench-5 score, respectively. On the distilled
four-evaluation model, it provides an additional \(2.13\times\) acceleration
while retaining 99.58\% of the native distilled-model score. Component
analysis confirms the distinct roles of the proposed modules: ITU improves
cross-resolution reconstruction, TTDA reduces the transition-point endpoint
discrepancy, and scheduler-consistent state reconstruction supports stable
high-resolution continuation. These results demonstrate that learned
in-trajectory resolution scaling provides spatial acceleration complementary
to timestep distillation.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The lightweight modules in InTraScale remain specific to their deployment
configuration. ITU learns the cross-resolution latent correspondence for a
particular VAE and scaling factor, while TTDA is adapted to a specific
resolution transition evaluation. Extending InTraScale to a new backbone,
scaling factor, or transition point may therefore require regenerating
model-endogenous supervision and training the corresponding modules.
Moreover, the current framework uses a predefined resolution transition
schedule rather than dynamically allocating low- and high-resolution
computation according to the convergence state of each video. Future work
could develop transition modules shared across models and scaling factors,
together with content- and trajectory-aware strategies for selecting the
resolution transition point, and validate their generalization across a
broader range of video generators.
