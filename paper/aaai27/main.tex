\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{abstract}
High-resolution video diffusion is costly because every denoising step
processes long spatiotemporal latent sequences at the target resolution.
Since early steps mainly determine global structure and motion while
later steps recover details, mixed-resolution sampling can reduce
computation; however, resolution handoff must address residual
trajectory error, non-scale-equivariant VAE latents, and
high-resolution trajectory re-entry. We propose
\textbf{Trajectory-Aligned Latent Handoff (TALH)}, a lightweight
latent-space framework that performs most denoising at low resolution and
decomposes the handoff into three components: a LoRA-based
\textbf{Trajectory Alignment Adapter (TAA)} that calibrates the residual
trajectory gap; a \textbf{Clean Latent Lifter (CLL)} that maps clean
latents across resolutions; and \textbf{High-Resolution Trajectory Re-entry
(HTR)} to re-noise the lifted latent for continued high-resolution denoising.
Operating under a model-endogenous self-distillation paradigm, TAA and CLL
are trained from supervision generated by the frozen target model, requiring
no external paired videos or additional teacher. On 50-step Wan2.1 with a
\(368\times640 \rightarrow 720\times1248\) handoff, TALH-Q and TALH-E
achieve \(1.83\times\) and \(2.22\times\) end-to-end speedups while
retaining 97.76\% and 97.53\% of Native-HR VBench-5. Compared to trilinear
interpolation, CLL reduces latent \(L_1\) error by 48.6\% and LPIPS by
73.3\% for cross-resolution lifting, while TAA reduces endpoint \(L_1\)
error by 25.8\% and 21.0\% at steps 40 and 45. TALH consistently exhibits
a robust quality-efficiency Pareto frontier across both standard 50-step
and 4-step distilled samplers.
\end{abstract}
